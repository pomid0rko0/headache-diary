# Headache diary — output requirements (Stage 1)
Version: v07 · Iteration: 07 · Status: under review

## 1. Scope and exclusions

This document lists the requirements for the **outputs** of a headache diary: reports, summaries, measures, indicators, charts, clinician summaries and patient-facing overviews, grounded in evidence-based medicine.

Out of scope (PROCESS.md section 2), and therefore not described anywhere in this document:

1. **Input data**: what is entered or recorded, data fields, data-entry questions, entry frequency, reminders, the data-entry interface.
2. **Storage format**: data models, schemas, databases, file layouts, encodings, retention.
3. **Derivation rules**: functions `f(input) -> output` such as algorithms, formulas, counting procedures, decision logic or thresholds applied to recorded data.

Each requirement describes what an output is, who reads it, why the evidence requires it, and the observable properties of the output (unit, reporting period, granularity, comparisons it must allow, labelling, caveats). Where a standard fixes the clinical meaning of an output (for example ICHD-3 or an IHS trial guideline), the standard is named with its section or code instead of restating its criteria. Some quoted passages contain numeric criteria that belong to the cited source; they are quoted to show the clinical need for the output and are **not** requirements of this document. Technical delivery formats are out of scope.

## 2. Audiences

- **Patients (adults)** who keep the diary and use its outputs to understand their own headache pattern, medication use and treatment effect, and to prepare consultations.
- **Parents and caregivers** of children and adolescents with headache.
- **Primary-care clinicians** (general practitioners, community pharmacists, nurses) who diagnose, start and follow up treatment.
- **Neurologists and headache specialists**, including tertiary headache centres, who assess treatment response, medication overuse, resistant or refractory migraine and eligibility for specific treatments.
- **Researchers and registries** that use diary-based outcome measures aligned with international trial methodology.
- Clinicians preparing **reimbursement documentation** for national schemes that require diary-based measures (the outputs are read by the clinician; the scheme defines its own criteria).

## 3. Requirements

Requirements are grouped by topic. RFC 2119 words are used (MUST, SHOULD, MAY, SHOULD NOT). Location references give the section and the page of the PDF or web page at the URL listed in section 5.

**Group A — Reporting frame, labelling and reliability**

### OUT-001 Defined reporting periods that allow like-for-like comparison
- Statement: Every summary output MUST state the reporting period it covers (start date, end date and length). Frequency outputs MUST be available per calendar month or per 28-day (4-week) period, the periods used in ICHD-3 "Definitions of terms" [S-001] and in the IHS trial guidelines [S-002][S-005], and the output MUST allow comparison of periods of equal length (for example a pre-treatment baseline period against a later period of the same length).
- Audience: patients, primary-care clinicians, specialists, researchers.
- Rationale: The core measures of headache burden are defined per period ("commonly one month"), and treatment effects are judged by comparing periods. Comparisons are only interpretable when the compared periods are of equal length and clearly dated.
- Evidence basis: Classification standard (ICHD-3) and international trial-methodology guidelines (IHS); consensus-based. Strength: MUST.
- Evidence:
  - [S-001] "Headache days: Number of days during an observed period of time (commonly one month) affected by headache for any part or the whole of the day." (Definitions of terms, p. 210)
  - [S-001] "Frequency of attacks: The rate of occurrence of attacks of headache (or pain) (qv) per time period (commonly one month)." (Definitions of terms, p. 209)
  - [S-005] "The time periods for analyses of efficacy should be equivalent. Thus, if a baseline period is 28 days, the final 28 days of the treatment period should be assessed." (section "Primary endpoints", comments, p. 810)
  - [S-002] "The primary endpoint in controlled trials of preventive treatment of episodic migraine should be the change from baseline in migraine days per unit time." (section 1.3.1, recommendation a, p. 1035)
- Caveats: Calendar months differ in length; when calendar months are shown, the output should make the number of days in each period visible so that readers can judge comparability.

### OUT-002 Labelling of the definitions behind each measure
- Statement: Outputs that report migraine days, headache days, moderate/severe headache days or attacks SHOULD state which published definition of each measure they follow (for example ICHD-3 "Definitions of terms" [S-001], IHS trial guidelines [S-002][S-003], or the 2026 eDelphi consensus definition of a migraine day [S-043]).
- Audience: clinicians, researchers.
- Rationale: There is no single universally adopted definition of a migraine day; different definitions give different numbers. A reader can compare a diary value with guideline or trial values only if the definition used is visible.
- Evidence basis: International trial-methodology guideline (IHS) and expert consensus (eDelphi; Dutch national guideline working group). Strength: SHOULD.
- Evidence:
  - [S-003] "Because cross-study comparisons may be complicated by differences in how migraine and headache days are defined, it is critical that these endpoints be prospectively defined." (section 1.3.1, comments, p. 823)
  - [S-043] "Although guidelines for controlled trials have proposed a definition for a migraine day, substantial variability remains across clinical trials, highlighting the need for a universally adopted definition." (Abstract, Background)
  - [S-027] "Hierbij moet opgemerkt worden dat de definitie van een migrainepatiënt op basis van ICHD-3 criteria is vastgesteld terwijl er nog geen (inter)nationale consensus lijkt te zijn over de definitie van een migraine dag" (module "Digitale dagboeken", Overwegingen). Translation: "It should be noted that the definition of a migraine patient is established on the basis of ICHD-3 criteria, whereas there does not yet appear to be (inter)national consensus on the definition of a migraine day"

### OUT-003 Completeness indicator for each reporting period
- Statement: Each reporting period SHOULD be accompanied by an indicator of how completely the period is covered by diary information (for example expressed as the proportion of days of the period that the diary covers), shown next to the summary measures so that readers can judge their reliability. The output SHOULD NOT present a fixed completeness cut-off as clinically validated.
- Audience: clinicians, researchers, patients.
- Rationale: Missing diary days bias frequency measures. Trial methodology treats diary compliance as something to be assessed, a regulator anticipates missing diary data in longer studies, a national guideline working group judged that less-than-complete diaries can still give sufficient insight, and a consensus on refractory migraine requires completed diaries.
- Evidence basis: International trial-methodology guideline (IHS), regulatory draft guidance (EMA), national guideline (NVN, very low certainty of evidence, working-group opinion), consensus (EHF). Strength: SHOULD.
- Evidence:
  - [S-002] "Use of a diary during the baseline phase is also important for assessing [...] subject compliance with the diary." (section 1.1.4, comments, p. 1029)
  - [S-022] "As trials for prophylactic treatment are longer than trials for acute treatment, it is possible that a missing data problem will arise due to missing diary entries or patients discontinuing the study." (section 5.2.5.2, lines 464–465, p. 13)
  - [S-027] "De werkgroep verwacht dat niet alle patiënten 100% respons kunnen halen, maar 80% respons rate zal voldoende inzicht geven." (module "Digitale dagboeken", Waarden en voorkeuren van patiënten). Translation: "The working group expects that not all patients can achieve a 100% response, but an 80% response rate will give sufficient insight."
  - [S-013] "For refractory migraine, chart review alone is not sufficient and a minimum period of observation of 6 months is required together with completed diaries." (Results, paragraph beginning "Refractory migraine is defined", the third paragraph after Table 3 "Proposed updated definition of resistant and refractory migraine")
- Caveats: The 80% figure in [S-027] is working-group opinion, and trial exclusion rules for incomplete diaries are designed for trials; neither is a validated clinical cut-off.

**Group B — Headache frequency and its course over time**

### OUT-004 Headache days per reporting period
- Statement: The diary output MUST report the number of headache days per reporting period, with "headache days" as defined in ICHD-3 "Definitions of terms" [S-001], shown for every period in the selected time range.
- Audience: patients, primary-care clinicians, specialists, researchers.
- Rationale: Monthly headache days underlie the classification of chronic headache disorders, the choice to start preventive treatment and the assessment of its effect. A graded recommendation (Japan) names headache days as the first information a headache diary provides, and national guidelines in several languages name headache days as the core diary measure.
- Evidence basis: Classification standard (ICHD-3); graded national guideline (Japan: strong recommendation, certainty of evidence B); national guidelines and consensus (Russia; SFEMC, France; DMKG/ÖKSG/SKG). Strength: MUST.
- Evidence:
  - [S-001] "Headache days: Number of days during an observed period of time (commonly one month) affected by headache for any part or the whole of the day." (Definitions of terms, p. 210)
  - [S-031] "頭痛ダイアリーからは頭痛日数，服薬日数，治療効果など，頭痛診療を行ううえで多くの情報を得ることができる．" (CQ I-10, recommendation, p. 32) Translation: "From a headache diary, much information for headache care can be obtained, such as the number of headache days, the number of medication days and the treatment effect." Grade shown in the source: "強い推奨/エビデンスの確実性 B" (strong recommendation / certainty of evidence B).
  - [S-033] "[...] помогает пациенту и врачу отличить один вариант ГБ от другого (например, приступ мигрени от эпизода ГБН), установить число дней с ГБ в месяц и число дней с приемом лекарственных препаратов с обезболивающим действием в месяц." (section 2.5, p. 26) Translation: "[Keeping a headache diary before the consultation] helps the patient and the physician to distinguish one type of headache from another (for example, a migraine attack from an episode of tension-type headache) and to establish the number of headache days per month and the number of days per month with intake of medicines with an analgesic effect."
  - [S-024] "l'agenda des crises, qui permet d'évaluer le nombre de jours de céphalées mensuels et l'utilisation de médicaments de crise" (section "Les outils", p. 400) Translation: "the attack diary, which makes it possible to evaluate the number of monthly headache days and the use of acute medication"
  - [S-023] "In jedem Fall ist eine verlässliche Dokumentation der Migränefrequenz, der Kopfschmerztage und des Bedarfs an Akutmedikation erforderlich." (section "Frage 3", final paragraph) Translation: "In every case, reliable documentation of migraine frequency, headache days and the need for acute medication is required."

### OUT-005 Migraine days per reporting period
- Statement: For patients with migraine, the diary output MUST report the number of migraine days per reporting period (monthly migraine days). The output SHOULD label the definition of a migraine day that it follows (see OUT-002).
- Audience: patients, clinicians, researchers.
- Rationale: Monthly migraine days are the primary efficacy endpoint of preventive migraine trials, the main parameter in the GRADE-based EHF guideline and in practice recommendations, and they express the migraine frequency that a graded guideline recommendation asks clinicians to document when assessing treatment efficacy.
- Evidence basis: Graded guideline recommendation (AAN/AHS 2026, recommendation 15A, level B); international trial-methodology guideline (IHS); GRADE-based guideline (EHF); regulatory draft guidance (EMA 2026; FDA 2023). Strength: MUST for reporting migraine days per reporting period; SHOULD for the definition label, whose evidence (OUT-002) is a trial-methodology comment, an eDelphi consensus and a guideline working-group consideration.
- Evidence:
  - [S-018] "15A. Clinicians should document the frequency of migraine attacks to optimize assessment of treatment efficacy." (Recommendation 15, level B, p. 8)
  - [S-002] "The primary endpoint in controlled trials of preventive treatment of episodic migraine should be the change from baseline in migraine days per unit time." (section 1.3.1, recommendation a, p. 1035)
  - [S-011] "The selected outcome parameters were reduction in monthly migraine days and proportion of individuals with migraine having at least a 50% reduction from baseline in monthly migraine days." (Methods)
  - [S-022] "The primary endpoint in confirmatory trials of preventive treatment for both episodic and chronic migraine should be the change from baseline in the mean number of monthly migraine days (MMDs)." (section 5.2.3, lines 393–394, p. 11)
  - [S-046] "In episodic migraine trials, the primary efficacy endpoint should assess the change from baseline in the number of monthly migraine days [...]." (section III.A.5 "Efficacy Endpoints", lines 163–164, p. 5)

### OUT-006 Moderate/severe headache days per reporting period
- Statement: The diary output SHOULD report the number of days with headache of moderate or severe intensity per reporting period, with the definition named as in OUT-002.
- Audience: clinicians, researchers, patients.
- Rationale: Moderate/severe headache days are an alternative primary endpoint in IHS trial methodology, part of the IHS practice success criteria, and part of the IHS absolute treatment goals. FDA draft guidance accepts monthly headache days of moderate/severe intensity (and/or requiring acute migraine medication) as a primary endpoint in chronic migraine trials.
- Evidence basis: International trial-methodology guideline (IHS), international practice recommendations (IHS, consensus) and regulatory draft guidance (FDA 2023, for chronic migraine trials). Strength: SHOULD.
- Evidence:
  - [S-002] "Alternative primary endpoints include change from baseline in moderate/severe headache days or 50% responder rate for the reduction of migraine days." (section 1.3.1, recommendation b, p. 1035)
  - [S-046] "For chronic migraine trials, sponsors may use the change from baseline in the number of monthly migraine days or in the number of monthly headache days (of moderate/severe intensity and/or requiring use of an acute migraine medication) as the primary efficacy endpoint." (section III.A.5 "Efficacy Endpoints", lines 164–167, p. 5)
  - [S-008] "a ≥ 50% decrease in monthly migraine days or moderate-to-severe headache days, optimally based on the use of a headache diary;" (Q6, Optimal-level recommendation, p. 12)
  - [S-010] "In this Position Statement, we advocate for raising the standards of migraine prevention by setting ambitious treatment goals aimed at optimal outcomes, such as migraine freedom or very low number of days with migraine or moderate/severe headache." (Abstract)

### OUT-007 Number of attacks per reporting period
- Statement: The diary output SHOULD report the number of attacks per reporting period separately from the number of headache or migraine days, with "frequency of attacks" as defined in ICHD-3 "Definitions of terms" [S-001].
- Audience: clinicians, researchers.
- Rationale: Attack frequency and days with headache are distinct measures; IHS trial methodology gives reduction in migraine attacks the highest priority among secondary outcomes, and the EMA guideline in force uses attack frequency as the primary prophylaxis endpoint.
- Evidence basis: Classification standard (ICHD-3 definitions); international trial-methodology guideline (IHS); regulatory guidance (EMA, 2007, in force). Strength: SHOULD.
- Evidence:
  - [S-001] "Frequency of attacks: The rate of occurrence of attacks of headache (or pain) (qv) per time period (commonly one month)." (Definitions of terms, p. 209)
  - [S-002] "The secondary outcome with highest priority is the reduction in migraine attacks." (section 1.3.2.0, p. 1035)
  - [S-021] "The recommended primary endpoint is the frequency of attacks within a pre-specified period, e.g., the mean frequency of attacks per 4 weeks or during the final 4 weeks of a 3-month study duration." (section "Migraine prophylaxis trials", p. 5)
- Caveats: The draft revision of the EMA guideline [S-022] moves the primary prophylaxis endpoint to monthly migraine days; attack counts remain useful as a secondary measure.

### OUT-008 Headache-free and symptom-free days
- Statement: The diary output MAY report headache-free days and symptom-free days per reporting period, and periods without migraine days, as exploratory measures of residual burden.
- Audience: patients, clinicians, researchers.
- Rationale: IHS trial guidelines list symptom-free days as exploratory outcomes, and the IHS position statement sets migraine freedom as the aspirational goal of prevention.
- Evidence basis: International trial-methodology guidelines (exploratory outcomes) and IHS position statement (expert consensus). Strength: MAY.
- Evidence:
  - [S-002] "Exploratory outcome measures can be used to capture outcomes that may be clinically meaningful and correlate with primary/other secondary endpoints." (section 1.3.2.5, p. 1037)
  - [S-002] "Symptom-free days are defined as the days free of premonitory, aura, headache, and postdromal symptoms." (section 1.3.2.5.1, p. 1037)
  - [S-010] "In this Position Statement, we advocate for raising the standards of migraine prevention by setting ambitious treatment goals aimed at optimal outcomes, such as migraine freedom or very low number of days with migraine or moderate/severe headache." (Abstract)

### OUT-009 Time-course view of frequency measures
- Statement: The diary output SHOULD show frequency measures (OUT-004 to OUT-007) as a time series of consecutive reporting periods (for example a chart of consecutive months or 28-day periods), with dated markers for the start, dose change and stop of preventive treatments, so that onset of effect, the trajectory of response and spontaneous fluctuation can be seen. Where weekly granularity is clinically relevant (tension-type headache, cluster headache, early onset of effect), weekly values SHOULD be available.
- Audience: patients, clinicians, researchers.
- Rationale: Migraine fluctuates spontaneously; the onset and trajectory of treatment effect matter for management; primary-care guidance recommends calendars for "charting outcomes"; IHS methodology recommends presenting efficacy measures at regular intervals to show onset and change of effect over time.
- Evidence basis: International trial-methodology guidelines (IHS); expert/consensus guidance (Lifting The Burden/EHF; IHS practice recommendations). Strength: SHOULD.
- Evidence:
  - [S-014] "recording treatment effect on headache frequency, and charting outcomes." (section "Headache diary and calendar to aid diagnosis and follow-up in primary care")
  - [S-005] "As a secondary endpoint, each 28-day interval can also be compared with baseline to demonstrate the trajectory of response." (section "Primary endpoints", comments, p. 810)
  - [S-006] "It is recommended that the efficacy measures are presented at regular intervals, e.g. for each week, during the trial as secondary efficacy measures, to give an impression of onset of effect and of possible increased or diminished effect with time." (section 2.3.3.1, comments, p. 10)
  - [S-002] "Understanding the onset of action of a preventive treatment may help to refine management strategies." (section 1.3.2.1.7, p. 1036)
  - [S-008] "In addition, migraine is a cyclic and fluctuating disease, and the frequency of migraine attacks changes depending on several different factors (44)." (Q2, Evidence, p. 9)

**Group C — Severity, duration, associated symptoms and aura**

### OUT-010 Headache intensity summary
- Statement: The diary output SHOULD summarise headache intensity per reporting period (for example the distribution of headache days by peak intensity category, or an average peak intensity on the scale used), on a stated scale, and allow comparison between periods.
- Audience: clinicians, patients.
- Rationale: Guidelines treat a reduction in severity as a treatment success in its own right; modest changes in intensity are clinically relevant for patients; a graded recommendation lists severity among the dimensions for assessing efficacy.
- Evidence basis: Graded guideline recommendation (AAN/AHS 2026, 15D, level B); national guidelines (SIGN; Danish reference programme; NVN); international trial-methodology guideline (IHS, tension-type headache). Strength: SHOULD.
- Evidence:
  - [S-018] "15D. Clinicians should utilize frequency, severity, migraine-associated symptoms, QOL, and use of acute therapy when assessing efficacy of treatment." (Recommendation 15, level B, p. 8)
  - [S-016] "Modest improvements in the frequency or severity of migraine headaches may provide considerable benefits to an individual." (section 4.1, p. 14)
  - [S-030] "Profylaktisk behandling anses generelt for succesfuld, hvis hyppigheden eller styrken af migræne kan halveres uden at der forekommer for generende bivirkninger." (migraine chapter, preventive treatment, PDF p. 19) Translation: "Prophylactic treatment is generally considered successful if the frequency or intensity of migraine can be halved without excessively bothersome side effects occurring."
  - [S-029] "Zo kan vermindering in ernst van de migraineaanvallen en verbetering in kwaliteit van leven zwaar meewegen in de behandelkeuze." (module "Monoklonale antilichamen", Overwegingen) Translation: "Thus a reduction in the severity of migraine attacks and an improvement in quality of life can weigh heavily in the treatment choice."
  - [S-006] "This is supported by clinical experience indicating that a modest reduction in headache intensity and duration is considered highly relevant and a major improvement for patients with CTTH." (section 2.3.3.2, comments, p. 10)

### OUT-011 Attack and headache duration summary
- Statement: The diary output SHOULD summarise attack or headache duration per reporting period (for example median attack duration, or hours with moderate/severe pain per 28 days), and SHOULD label duration measures for acutely treated attacks as influenced by acute treatment.
- Audience: clinicians, patients.
- Rationale: Several national guidelines name duration as a target and an efficacy indicator of prevention; IHS methodology lists cumulative hours of moderate/severe pain as potentially clinically meaningful; regulators consider duration of treated attacks unreliable, which must be shown as a caveat.
- Evidence basis: National guidelines and consensus (China; Turkey; Saudi Arabia; DMKG/ÖKSG/SKG); international trial-methodology guideline (IHS). Evidence is conflicting (EMA). Strength: SHOULD.
- Evidence:
  - [S-032] "主要包括偏头痛发作频率、头痛持续时间、头痛程度、头痛的功能损害程度及对急性期治疗的反应。" (section 3.2 "预防性治疗有效性指标") Translation: "[Indicators of the effectiveness of preventive treatment] mainly include migraine attack frequency, headache duration, headache severity, the degree of headache-related functional impairment and the response to acute treatment."
  - [S-042] "Significant decrease in attack duration based on patient’s headache diary" (Question 6, consensus statement)
  - [S-035] "Profilaktik tedavinin başarısı, atak sıklığında ve süresinde %50'lik bir azalma ve akut atakta ilaçlara artan yanıt ile değerlendirilir." (section 3.6.2 "Farmakolojik Tedavi Stratejileri: Profilaktik Tedavi", bulleted list of preventive treatment principles directly below Kutu 8 "Migren Profilaktik Tedavi-Yönetim-6", PDF p. 48) Translation: "The success of prophylactic treatment is assessed by a 50% reduction in attack frequency and duration and an increased response to drugs in the acute attack."
  - [S-023] "Dabei ist es Ziel der medikamentösen und nichtmedikamentösen Prophylaxe, die Frequenz, Intensität und Dauer von Migräneattacken zu reduzieren." (section "Hintergrund") Translation: "The aim of pharmacological and non-pharmacological prophylaxis is to reduce the frequency, intensity and duration of migraine attacks."
  - [S-002] "Cumulative hours per 28 days of moderate/severe pain [...] and reductions in this endpoint may be clinically meaningful." (section 1.3.2.1.6, p. 1036)
- Caveats: Conflicting evidence: [S-021] "The duration of attacks is not considered a reliable secondary endpoint as it is heavily influenced by the use of rescue medication, which is allowed after 2 hours." (section "Acute migraine attack trials", p. 5). The output should therefore not present duration of treated attacks as a stand-alone efficacy measure.

### OUT-012 Associated symptoms and most bothersome symptom
- Statement: For migraine, the diary output SHOULD report the presence of migraine-associated symptoms (nausea/vomiting, photophobia, phonophobia) and the patient's most bothersome symptom, both across periods and in the response of individual treated attacks (see OUT-023).
- Audience: clinicians, researchers, patients.
- Rationale: Regulators and IHS methodology regard pain alone as insufficient to describe acute treatment effect; the most bothersome symptom is a co-primary acute endpoint; a graded recommendation lists migraine-associated symptoms among efficacy dimensions; EHF defines effective acute treatment to include relief of non-pain symptoms.
- Evidence basis: Graded guideline recommendation (AAN/AHS 2026, 15D, level B); regulatory guidance (FDA 2018); international trial-methodology guideline (IHS 2019); consensus (EHF 2022). Strength: SHOULD.
- Evidence:
  - [S-020] "Because migraine is a complex disorder characterized by several associated symptoms (i.e., nausea, photophobia, and phonophobia) in addition to headache, a drug effect on headache pain alone is not considered sufficient to grant a claim for the acute treatment of migraine." (section III.B.5, p. 4)
  - [S-004] "To align outcomes in controlled trials with symptom(s) of clinical significance, it is important to measure the effects of an acute treatment on all these features." (section 1.3.3, comments)
  - [S-012] "The Consensus Panel agreed that not only pain, but also non-pain symptoms contribute to the satisfaction of individuals with migraine with their acute treatments." (Discussion)
  - [S-018] "15D. Clinicians should utilize frequency, severity, migraine-associated symptoms, QOL, and use of acute therapy when assessing efficacy of treatment." (Recommendation 15, level B, p. 8)

### OUT-013 Aura frequency and duration
- Statement: For patients with migraine with aura, the diary output MAY report the number of auras per reporting period and their duration, separately from headache measures.
- Audience: clinicians, patients.
- Rationale: The French guideline sets reduction of aura frequency, duration and severity as an aim of prophylaxis in migraine with aura, and ICHD-3 notes that an aura diary may clarify the diagnosis; the evidence base for aura-directed treatment is limited.
- Evidence basis: National guideline (SFEMC, France); classification standard comment (ICHD-3). Evidence for aura-specific treatment effects is weak. Strength: MAY.
- Evidence:
  - [S-025] "Pour la migraine avec aura, l'objectif est également de réduire la fréquence, la durée et la gravité des auras, mais on manque d'études de qualité sur l'efficacité des médicaments spécifiquement destinés à cet effet." (section "Quels sont les objectifs du traitement prophylactique de la migraine ?", p. 413) Translation: "For migraine with aura, the aim is also to reduce the frequency, duration and severity of auras, but there is a lack of good-quality studies on the efficacy of drugs specifically intended for this purpose."
  - [S-001] "After an initial consultation, use of an aura diary may clarify the diagnosis." (1.2 Migraine with aura, comments, end of the paragraph beginning "Patients often find it hard to describe their aura symptoms", p. 21)

**Group D — Acute medication use and medication overuse**

### OUT-014 Acute medication days by drug class and by drug
- Statement: The diary output MUST report, per reporting period, the number of days with acute (symptomatic) headache medication, broken down by drug class as used in ICHD-3 code 8.2 and its subtypes [S-001] (for example ergotamine, triptans, simple analgesics, opioids, combination analgesics, and other classes) and by individual drug, together with the total across classes.
- Audience: clinicians, patients, researchers.
- Rationale: ICHD-3 distinguishes medication-overuse headache by drug class and stresses that a diary allows precise judgement of medication consumption; reducing acute medication use is an explicit aim of prevention, and FDA draft guidance names the change in days per month with acute medication use as a secondary efficacy endpoint of preventive trials; a graded recommendation lists use of acute therapy among efficacy dimensions; national guidelines in many languages name medication days as a core diary output.
- Evidence basis: Classification standard (ICHD-3); graded guideline recommendations (AAN/AHS 2026, 15D, level B; Japan CQ I-10, strong recommendation, certainty of evidence B); international trial-methodology guideline (IHS); regulatory draft guidance (FDA 2023); national guidelines (SEN 2026, Spain, a statement without a level of evidence or grade of recommendation; Russia; France). Strength: MUST.
- Evidence:
  - [S-001] "It has been shown that such a headache diary improves diagnostic accuracy as well as allowing a more precise judgement of medication consumption." (How to use this classification, item 12, p. 7)
  - [S-002] "Reducing the use of medication for acute treatment in individuals who experience frequent migraine attacks can decrease the risk of medication-overuse headache (MOH) and progression from episodic migraine to chronic migraine" (section 1, p. 1028)
  - [S-018] "15D. Clinicians should utilize frequency, severity, migraine-associated symptoms, QOL, and use of acute therapy when assessing efficacy of treatment." (Recommendation 15, level B, p. 8)
  - [S-031] "頭痛ダイアリーからは頭痛日数，服薬日数，治療効果など，頭痛診療を行ううえで多くの情報を得ることができる．" (CQ I-10, recommendation, p. 32; strong recommendation, certainty of evidence B) Translation: "From a headache diary, much information for headache care can be obtained, such as the number of headache days, the number of medication days and the treatment effect."
  - [S-046] "Change from baseline in the number of days per month subjects use an acute/rescue medication to treat an acute migraine" (section III.A.5 "Efficacy Endpoints", secondary efficacy endpoint a, lines 174–175, p. 5)
  - [S-045] "Debe promoverse el uso de diarios para monitorizar su cefalea y el uso de medicación." (chapter 11 "Otras cefaleas secundarias", section 5.4 "Cefalea por uso excesivo de medicación", subsection 5.4.7.1 "Educación y otras terapias", PDF p. 395, printed p. 393; no level of evidence or grade of recommendation is attached) Translation: "The use of diaries to monitor their headache and medication use should be promoted."
  - [S-033] "Важным является определение типа препаратов злоупотребления и установление «числа дней с приемом лекарственных препаратов, обладающих обезболивающим действием, в месяц» - главного диагностического критерия ЛИГБ (табл. 4б)." (section 2, comment to table 4а "diagnostic criteria of medication-overuse headache", p. 19) Translation: "It is important to determine the type of overused medicine and to establish the 'number of days per month with intake of medicines with an analgesic effect', the main diagnostic criterion of medication-overuse headache (table 4b)."
  - [S-024] "À chaque visite, il est recommandé de revoir le traitement de crise, à savoir le type de médicament, le nombre de jours de prise (calendrier des maux de tête), la tolérance, la posologie, le moment et la voie d'administration, ainsi que le respect des contre-indications." ### OUT-015 Weekly view of acute medication use
- Statement: In addition to per-period totals (OUT-014), the diary output SHOULD show acute medication days per calendar week, so that use on several days within a week is visible.
- Audience: clinicians, patients.
- Rationale: IHS practice recommendations express limits for acute medication use both per week and per month, and a national guideline associates acute treatment on more than two days per week with medication overuse; a monthly total alone hides clustering within weeks.
- Evidence basis: International practice recommendations (IHS 2024, Q12 "Optimal" recommendation; consensus based on guidelines); national guideline (BASH V2.0 2026). Strength: SHOULD.
- Evidence:

### OUT-016 Medication-overuse indicator referenced to ICHD-3 code 8.2

### OUT-033 Results of comorbidity screening instruments

- Audience: clinicians.
- Rationale: Depression and anxiety are common in migraine and are risk factors for chronification; the French guideline suggests HADS as an assessment tool.
- Evidence basis: National guideline (SFEMC, France, suggestion); international trial-methodology guideline (IHS). Strength: MAY.
- Evidence:
  - [S-024] "l'échelle HAD, qui évalue les comorbidités psychiatriques." (section "Les outils", p. 400) Translation: "the HAD scale, which assesses psychiatric comorbidities."
  - [S-002] "Major depression, anxiety, obesity, low back pain, and sleep apnoea are common in patients with episodic migraine (37), and psychiatric comorbidities and allodynia are risk factors for progression from episodic to chronic migraine (38,39)." (section 1.1.9, comments, p. 1031)

### OUT-036 Paediatric outputs
- Statement: For children and adolescents, the diary output SHOULD (a) report PedMIDAS results with their 3-month recall period, (b) report school days missed or impaired and the impact on parents or caregivers (for example their lost productive time), (c) apply equal-length period comparisons as in OUT-001, and (d) present efficacy of treatment from diary data as recommended in paediatric guidance.

**Group J — Presentation constraints**

### OUT-037 No composite headache index as a primary measure
- Statement: The diary output SHOULD NOT present a composite headache index that multiplies or otherwise combines frequency, intensity and/or duration into one score as a primary measure. If such an index is shown, it SHOULD be labelled as exploratory and shown together with its component measures.
- Audience: clinicians, patients, researchers.
- Rationale: Regulatory guidance and IHS methodology warn that composite indices weight arbitrary scales, cannot be compared meaningfully between people, are hard to interpret clinically and can mask changes in individual domains.
- Evidence basis: Regulatory guidance (EMA 2007, in force; EMA draft revision 2026); international trial-methodology guideline (IHS TTH 2010). Strength: SHOULD NOT.
- Evidence:
  - [S-021] "Headache indices multiplying frequency, intensity and/or duration are not recommended, as faulty weighting in the arbitrary numerical scores will be increased by multiplication and, most important, indices can in no meaningful way be compared between subjects. Furthermore, results are difficult to evaluate clinically." (section "Migraine prophylaxis trials", p. 6)
  - [S-022] "Headache indices encompassing frequency, intensity and/or duration into a single composite score are difficult to interpret in terms of clinical relevance of observed changes of the score given that such aggregation can mask the clinical relevance of changes observed in individual domains." (section 5.2.3, lines 421–423, p. 12)
  - [S-022] "Unless specifically validated as an informative endpoint, combined headache indices will only provide exploratory information." (section 5.2.3, lines 423–425, p. 12)
  - [S-006] "[...] when used in headache indices, faulty weighting in the arbitrary numerical intensity score will be increased by multiplication." (section 2.3.3.2, comments, p. 10)

### OUT-038 Interpretive caveats on every summary
- Statement: Summary outputs SHOULD state that they summarise patient-reported diary information, that they support but do not replace clinical assessment and diagnosis, and that indicators such as possible medication overuse (OUT-016), responder labels (OUT-020) and trigger associations (OUT-032) require clinical interpretation.
- Audience: patients, clinicians.
- Rationale: Diagnosis is made by the clinician; overuse of acute medication does not equal medication-overuse headache; national guidance notes that the headache diary is not a generally accepted validated method.
- Evidence basis: National guidelines (SIGN 155; NVN; Russian paediatric clinical recommendations). Strength: SHOULD.
- Evidence:
  - [S-016] "Importantly, not all patients overusing acute treatments have MOH and some just have poorly-treated migraine." (section 5, p. 30)
  - [S-027] "Het missen van sommige patiënten (vals-negatieven) met migraine zal geen grote gevolgen hebben, omdat de juiste diagnose vooralsnog door de arts zal worden gesteld" (module "Digitale dagboeken", Overwegingen) Translation: "Missing some patients (false negatives) with migraine will not have major consequences, because for the time being the correct diagnosis will be made by the physician"
  - [S-034] "Дневник головной боли не является общепринятым валидизированным методом, но при этом ведение Дневника головной боли позволяет улучшить контроль за самочувствием пациента, помогает лечащему врачу определить частоту, время возникновения, продолжительность приступов, факторы, вызывающие головную боль, а также влияние проводимой терапии на ГБ." (appendix А3 "Дневник головной боли", p. 50) Translation: "The headache diary is not a generally accepted validated method, but keeping a headache diary makes it possible to improve monitoring of the patient's well-being and helps the treating physician to determine the frequency, time of onset and duration of attacks, factors provoking the headache, and the influence of the therapy on the headache."
